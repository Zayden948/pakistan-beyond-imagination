02 — Orientation

Meet the land of the Indus

Cradled between South and Central Asia, Pakistan is one of humanity's oldest crossroads — where traders, monks, emperors and armies moved along the Indus and the Silk Road, and where their marks still stand in brick, marble and song.

Geography. Five great mountain ranges meet here — the Himalaya, Karakoram (home of K2, the second-highest peak on Earth), Hindu Kush, Sulaiman and Salt Range — while the Indus River carries their water 3,180 km down to the Arabian Sea.

Peoples & languages. Over 240 million people from Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashtun, Baloch, Kashmiri and Hunza communities speak more than seventy languages, from Urdu and Punjabi to Balti and Shina.

Landscapes. Desert dunes, alpine meadows, turquoise lakes, mangrove coasts and glaciers — K2, Deosai's highland plateau, Attabad's lake and the Thar desert all share one border.

Historical weight. The oldest known settled farming village (Mehrgarh), the Indus Valley cities, the Gandharan Buddhist universities, Mughal Lahore and the 1947 independence of one of the world's largest countries all happened here.

03 — The Urban Mosaic

Incredible cities

Seven worlds in one country — each carrying its own accent, craft and skyline.

Punjab · cultural capital

Lahore

Mughal domes over colonial arcades, bazaars that never sleep and gardens built for emperors — Lahore is Pakistan's beating heart.

  • Badshahi Mosque
  • Lahore Fort
  • Walled City
  • Shalimar Gardens

Walk the Walled City from Delhi Gate past Wazir Khan's mosque, then climb to the Fort's Sheesh Mahal — a hall of mirrors built for Shah Jahan. At dusk the Badshahi Mosque turns honey-gold, and the whole old quarter gathers to eat along the Food Street at Fort Road.

Best from October to March.

Islamabad Capital Territory · the modern capital

Islamabad

A capital planned among hills, organised around green avenues and crowned by the Faisal Mosque's desert-tent geometry.

  • Faisal Mosque
  • Margalla Hills
  • Saidpur Village

Trails from Trail 3 and Trail 5 climb the Margalla Hills above the city; at their foot sits Saidpur, a 400-year-old village absorbed into the capital. Faisal Mosque, designed by Vedat Dalokay, seats 100,000 worshippers under eight-fold concrete "tents".

Spring and late autumn are best.

Sindh · the city of the sea

Karachi

Pakistan's restless megacity — a 20-million-strong port where Mughal-era quawwalis meet neon and the Arabian Sea wind.

  • Clifton & Sea View
  • Mazar-e-Quaid
  • Empress Market
  • Frere Hall

Sunset drives down Marine Drive, biryani that people argue about across provinces, the white dome of Mazar-e-Quaid, and Burns Road's food lane. Karachi is every Pakistani region compressed into one coast.

Visit November to February for milder air.

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa · gateway to the Passes

Peshawar

Over two millennia old — the bazaar of story-tellers, where Silk Road caravans once parked below brick balconies.

  • Qissa Khwani Bazaar
  • Bala Hissar
  • Mahabat Khan Mosque
  • Khyber Pass

Qissa Khwani — "the Bazaar of Storytellers" — still draws crowds over chai and chapli kebabs. Nearby stand the Buddhist ruins of Takht-i-Bahi and the Khyber Pass, the corridor between the subcontinent and Central Asia.

Best seen in the cooler months.

Punjab · the city of saints

Multan

A Sufi capital of domed shrines and blue-glazed pottery, alive with qawwali and mango orchards.

  • Shah Rukn-e-Alam
  • Bahauddin Zakariya
  • Blue pottery
  • Sufi music

The towering 14th-century mausoleum of Shah Rukn-e-Alam rises above the old city in turquoise brick. The bazaars sell the famous blue pottery, camel-skin lamps and sohan halwa — Multan's slow, sweet signature.

Come for the Urs festivals in spring.

Gilgit-Baltistan · the valley kingdom

Hunza

Apricot orchards, stone-and-timber forts and a lake the colour of glass — Hunza feels like another world at 2,500 metres.

  • Baltit Fort
  • Attabad Lake
  • Rakaposhi
  • Passu Cones

Karimabad stacks itself below 700-year-old Baltit Fort. The Karakoram Highway threads past Attabad Lake's electric-blue water to the Passu Cones. Autumn paints the whole valley orange, and Burushaski — a language related to none other — fills the evening air.

Spring blossom and autumn harvest are the peaks.

Gilgit-Baltistan · gateway to the giants

Skardu

Where the Karakoram gathers its highest names — treks to K2 and Concordia begin from this high-desert town.

  • Shigar Fort
  • Deosai Plains
  • Satpara Lake
  • Cold desert

A 400-year-old fort turned heritage hotel sits in Shigar, upstream from lakes and dunes that touch snowline. The road north leads to Askole, the last village before the Baltoro Glacier and the great trek to K2 Base Camp.

Season runs June to September.

04 — Built Memory

Landmarks & monuments

Five thousand years leave architecture behind. These are the rooms where history still lives.

01

Badshahi Mosque

Lahore · 1673 · Mughal

The imperial mosque of Aurangzeb — red sandstone, marble inlay and one of the largest courtyards on Earth, filled with 60,000 worshippers at a time.

02

Faisal Mosque

Islamabad · 1986 · Modern Islamic

A desert Bedouin tent in white concrete, holding 100,000 faithful beneath the Margalla Hills — Islam's sixth-largest mosque.

03

Lahore Fort

Lahore · UNESCO · 11th–17th c.

Eleven centuries of palaces layered into one citadel — its Sheesh Mahal, the Hall of Mirrors, remains Mughal artistry at its most delicate.

04

Minar-e-Pakistan

Lahore · 1968 · Modern

Rising where the Lahore Resolution was passed in 1940 — the moment the demand for Pakistan was set down in writing.

05

Pakistan Monument

Islamabad · 2007 · Contemporary

Four flowering granite petals gather around a star — each petal a province, together forming the nation's own shape.

06

Mohenjo-daro

Sindh · UNESCO · c. 2500 BCE

The Mound of the Dead — an Indus Valley metropolis with gridded streets, covered sewers and a public bath, older than Rome by two millennia.

07

Rohtas Fort

Punjab · UNESCO · 1541

Sher Shah Suri's mountain fortress — four kilometres of wall, twelve gates, never taken by force.

08

Makli Necropolis

Sindh · UNESCO · 1352–1800

Half a million tombs spread across a Sindh hillside — one of the largest funerary landscapes ever built.

05 — The Wild North & Beyond

Natural wonders

Three of the world's longest glaciers outside the polar belts, a second-highest peak, a lake born of a landside and a plateau of wildflowers at 4,100 metres.

06 — The Long Story

A civilisation timeline

c. 3300–1300 BCE

The Indus Valley Civilisation

Along the Indus, one of humanity's first urban civilisations rose: Harappa and Mohenjo-daro built grid-planned streets, covered drains and private wells two thousand years before Rome existed. Their still-undeciphered script is one of archaeology's great open questions.

  • Mehrgarh, near Sibi, shows settled farming even earlier — from about 7000 BCE.
  • Mohenjo-daro may have held some 40,000 people at its height.
  • Standardised bricks and weights show remarkably unified city-planning.

07 — The Living Fabric

Culture, craft & colour

Language, dress, music and making — the daily rituals of a mosaic society.

Truck Art

A moving folk museum: hand-painted peacocks, calligraphy and chrome — the country's roads rolled forward.

Qawwali

Sufi devotional music from the shrines of Multan to Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan's world stages.

Dress

Shalwar Kameez

The national dress — flowing tunic and trouser, from everyday cottons to bridal silks heavy with tilla and mirror-work.

Worn across all provinces; the Ajrak block-print of Sindh is its most recognised pattern.

Festivals

A year in colour

Eid's new moons, Basant's skies of kites over Lahore, Mela Chiraghan's lamps at Hiran Minar, Urs festivals at Multan's shrines, and polo on horseback at Shandur — the world's highest ground.

Shandur sits at 3,700 m between Gilgit and Chitral.

Languages

One nation, seventy tongues

Urdu links the country; Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto, Saraiki, Balochi, Kashmiri, Shina, Balti and more carry their regions' poetry, proverbs and songs.

Radio, music and TV remain daily stages for multilingual culture.

The Regional Mosaic

Punjabi hearths, Sindhi ajrak, Pashtun hospitality, Baloch nomadic weaving, Kashmiri carpets, Hunza's harvest festivals — diversity as a daily condition, not a slogan.

Crafts

Made by hand

Multan's blue pottery, Sindh's ajrak and susi weaving, Chitral's Peshawari chappal… hand-knotted carpets, camel-skin lamps with calligraphic light, brassware from Walled City workshops.

Many crafts still descend from Mughal-era guilds.

Literature & thought

Iqbal, Bulleh Shah & the Sufis

Allama Iqbal's philosophy of khudi, Bulleh Shah's verses sung at every shrine, Punjabi and Sindhi poetry as living practice — recited, sung, woven into everyday language.

Iqbal's tomb sits by the Badshahi Mosque in Lahore.

Soundtrack

Folk to Coke Studio

Saraiki songs over the Radio Pakistan airwaves, Pashto rubab and dambura, Hunza's drum-and-dance, all remixed on Coke Studio stages that reach hundreds of millions.

The rubab sits at the heart of Pashtun music.

08 — The Kitchen of the Crossroads

Pakistani cuisine

Slow-cooked stews, dum biryanis, charred kebabs and ghee-fried bread — recipes at the meeting point of Mughal, Afghan, Persian and Sindh kitchens.

Biryani

Sindh · Karachi

Saffron-dyed basmati layered with spiced meat, mint and tomato — Pakistan's most-argued-over dish.

Nihari

Lahore · Karachi

Simmered overnight, served at dawn with naan, lemon and julienne ginger — the slow-food king.

Karahi

Nationwide

Cooked in the wok-shaped pan it's named for — tomato, green chilli and butter-flecked chicken or mutton.

Seekh Kebab

Nationwide

Minced meat hand-rolled around skewers, charred over coals — street food perfected.

Chapli Kebab

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

Peshawar's flat kebab — spicy minced meat pressed with tomato, pomegranate seed and animal fat for crunch.

Haleem

Nationwide

Wheat, lentils and meat stirred for eight hours into a silky paste — Ramadan's comfort food.

Sajji

Balochistan

Whole lamb or chicken roasted upright over coals with only salt and a green papaya paste — pure, elemental.

Paratha

Breakfast staple

Ghee-fried layered flatbread folded and rolled until flaky — the start of most mornings, with chai.
